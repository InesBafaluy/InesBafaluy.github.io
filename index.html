Since 2026École PolytechniqueTeam project

Policy in Action: Collective Agreements and the Green and Digital Transitions

With Unédic, we use NLP and econometrics to see how collective agreements on AI and the green transition shape the French labour market.

Read moreShow less

An applied research project with Unédic, the body that manages unemployment insurance in France. We study how collective agreements on artificial intelligence and the ecological transition support changes in the French labour market. The project uses NLP and LLMs to find and classify agreements published on Légifrance, links them to firm and employee data from Insee and Dares, and applies econometrics to employment, hiring and turnover, to inform the decisions of social partners.

  • Python
  • R
  • NLP
  • LLMs
  • Econometrics
  • Policy evaluation

Turbo Roundabouts vs. Classic Roundabouts

Studying traffic dynamics in turbo roundabouts through modeling and simulation.

Read moreShow less

A research project at INSA Toulouse on modeling traffic at roundabouts with the macroscopic LWR model. I built a simulation comparing conventional two-lane roundabouts with turbo roundabouts, using a Cell Transmission Model and gap-acceptance theory. Turbo roundabouts reduced congestion the most under intermediate to high traffic demand.

  • Python
  • LWR model
  • Cell Transmission Model
  • Gap acceptance

2025INSA ToulouseTeam project

Climate Change and Alpine Ecosystems

Modeling how climate change pushes mountain species uphill, from blueberry bushes to the marmots that eat them.

Read moreShow less

Population dynamics modeling with the Fisher-KPP reaction–diffusion equation, to simulate how species react when climate change shifts, shrinks or disrupts their habitat. The case study follows blueberry bushes and marmots in the mountains: as warming pushes the bushes uphill, the marmots follow them.

  • Python
  • PDEs
  • Fisher-KPP

2024UT AustinTeam project

Do Looks Win Votes? Faces and U.S. Elections

Can a machine predict elections from candidates' faces? We tested it, and looked closely at the biases.

Read moreShow less

A machine-learning project for my Intro to AI course at UT Austin. We mapped 68 facial landmarks on every U.S. presidential candidate since 1960, turned them into 2,280 distance features, and compared random forests, SVMs and neural networks to measure how facial features correlate with election results, while discussing the biases of such a model.
